Research

I have three current projects. With Alex Lazarian at UW-Madison and Dmitri Pogosyan at Alberta, I work on new statistical measures of interstellar magnetic turbulence from radio polarization and test them against MHD simulations. With Leonid Levitov at MIT, I study what happens when electrons in graphene stop acting like particles and start acting like a fluid that goes unstable. With Jim Fuller and Amirhossein Bagheri at Caltech, I ask whether Europa's ocean is as old as the moon or formed later, using a coupled thermal and orbital model. Earlier, from August 2025 to January 2026, I worked in Mark Vogelsberger's group at the MIT Kavli Institute on how self-interacting dark matter particles scatter near a supermassive black hole. In each case I do the math, write the simulations, and test the theory against them.

Publications

1. Recovering 3D Magnetic Turbulence from Single-Frequency Faraday Screens

The Astrophysical Journal 1011, 16 (2026). doi 10.3847/1538-4357/aeac22

Aliaksandr Melnichenka, Alex Lazarian, Dmitri Pogosyan

We demonstrate that three-dimensional magnetic turbulence statistics can be recovered from single-frequency radio polarimetry through the directional spectrum, an angle statistic introduced by Lazarian and Pogosyan. This method, validated against high-resolution magnetohydrodynamic simulations and controlled synthetic models, reliably extracts inertial-range scaling properties of turbulent magnetic fields. Comparative analysis with multi-frequency techniques establishes the conditions under which single-band observations suffice, providing observers with a practical framework for determining optimal frequency coverage requirements.

Fig. 4 of the paper. Directional spectrum Pu(k) from the 10243 AthenaK runs at sonic Mach 1 (red) and 10 (blue), without (solid) and with (dashed) Faraday rotation, against the k−11/3 reference.

2. Spontaneous Running Waves and Self-Oscillatory Transport in Dirac Fluids

arXiv 2512.16571 (In revision at Physical Review Letters)

Prayoga Liong*, Aliaksandr Melnichenka*, Anton Bukhtatyi, Albert Bilous, Leonid Levitov (* Equal contribution)

We demonstrate that ultra-clean electronic materials, such as graphene, exhibit hydrodynamic instabilities analogous to thin fluid films. Above a critical drive, the flow becomes unstable and develops self-sustained traveling waves. These waves manifest as coupled oscillations of electron density and current, producing both a non-analytic transition in the average current and a narrow-band, tunable high-frequency signal. This work establishes a new mechanism for generating and controlling high-frequency electronic oscillations through collective fluid-like behavior, distinct from conventional circuit-based approaches.

3. Numerical Tests of Directional Statistics and Polarization Zeros in Mixed Emitting and Faraday-Rotating Turbulence

In preparation for The Astrophysical Journal (2026)

Aliaksandr Melnichenka, Alex Lazarian, Dmitri Pogosyan

The published paper treats synchrotron emission and Faraday rotation as separate regions. This one tests the Lazarian and Pogosyan predictions for the harder case where they share the same volume, including the places where the polarization vanishes, on 10243 AthenaK cubes at sonic Mach 1 and 10 and on matched synthetic fields up to 20483, with the line of sight both along and across the mean field.

4. Has Europa Always Hosted an Ocean?

In preparation (2026)

Aliaksandr Melnichenka, Amirhossein Bagheri, Jim Fuller

Europa's ocean is a target in the search for life, and it is usually assumed to be as old as the moon. I added an ocean-free start and dehydration of the rock to Dr. Bagheri's coupled thermal and orbital model of Europa in the Laplace resonance, and explored its histories against what is measured today. The question is whether that ocean is primordial or billions of years younger, which NASA's Europa Clipper mission can help settle.

Code

AstroTurbulence. Python. Synthetic Faraday screens, the directional-spectrum estimator and the AthenaK map pipeline behind the ApJ paper.

electronic-kapitsa-waves. Python. Fourier pseudospectral solver for the one-dimensional hydrodynamic model of a Dirac fluid, with the parameter scans behind the Physical Review Letters manuscript.

SIDM_Transport_Theory_vs_MC. Python with Numba. Hénon velocity diffusion coefficients to third order, Monte Carlo validation, and an orbit-averaged loss-cone Monte Carlo after Shapiro and Marchant (1978).
